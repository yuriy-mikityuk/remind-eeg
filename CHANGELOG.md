# Changelog

Versions follow `muse_tmr.__version__`; the app shows the running version and build at the bottom of the page.

## Unreleased

### Recording

- The decoder keeps every ACCGYRO subpacket of a notification. It kept only the last one's three rows while `imu_samples` counted them all, so the motion features and the arousal guard got fewer IMU rows than the headband sent: 249 of 357 on `tests/test_data/muse_20250824_172756.bin`, where 34 of the 120 notifications carry two or three. Lost ACCGYRO subpackets are marked in `IMUSample.gaps` as EEG and PPG losses are, and the IMU features place the rows after a loss inside a frame that many samples later. Replaying `raw_amused.bin` gets every row back; a `decoded_frames.jsonl` written before lacks them. Synthetic nights now read the IMU out one, two or three subpackets at a time, in that capture's shares, so they carry such notifications too (#244).

### Local app

- The app, its reports and the macOS launcher are called REMind instead of Athena TMR. The launcher installer now puts `REMind.app` on the Desktop; an existing `Athena TMR.app` keeps working and can be deleted.
- The meditation guide says up front what the session is for, explains the mirrored block order without letters (a bracket under the two middle blocks, a drift drawing, why these two practices) and folds a one-picture version under its intro, in both languages. The meditation and series texts on the main page drop the ABBA/BAAB shorthand too.

### REM detection and cueing

- An arousal guard `lower_volume` lowers the volume that plays. The scheduler used to halve the cue's `volume_hint` and the player capped it afterwards, so under a calibration cap below the hint the halving was partly or wholly lost, and a cue without a hint was never lowered: a hint of 0.10 under a comfortable volume of 0.06 played at 0.05 instead of 0.03, and at 0.05 or less as loud as an unlowered cue. The player now takes the guard's multiplier (`AudioCuePlayer.play_test_cue(volume_multiplier=...)`) and applies it after every cap, so a lowered cue plays at exactly that fraction of its normal volume and never louder. Play events keep the cue's own `volume_hint` (`original_volume_hint` is gone) and, in Pilot 4 and 5, log `requested_volume`, `capped_volume`, `volume_multiplier` and `effective_volume`, as every line of `audio_playback.jsonl` now does; a lowered play adds the reason code `volume_lowered`. The audio log's other fields keep their values on `allow`, and `max_requested_volume` in the Pilot 4 and 5 summary is now the request before the guard. `train-tlr-cue` takes a hint of 0 as 0, like the nights, instead of the default volume (#255).
- On `p1034` the arousal guard lowers the volume on an epoch whose heart-rate series skipped more than half of its values: `ArousalGuardConfig.hr_rule_blind_action` defaults to `lower_volume` instead of `allow`, as the maintainer decided on #234. The decision is `lower_volume` with the reason code `hr_rule_blind`, so the TLR and puzzle cues of that epoch play at half their volume in Pilot 4 and 5, after the calibration cap since #255, and carry the multiplier in Pilot 3's plan. A pause or stop reason in the same epoch still wins, blind epochs in a row add up to no pause, `p21` never raises it, and the threshold stays at more than half. The HR-rule quality gates stay 0. On the four `p1034` scenarios at their own lengths 1 epoch of 678 changes, an N2 epoch of `battery_death_2h29` without a cue, and every cued play keeps its time and volume (synthetic input, not sleep); `agreement_baseline.json`, a `p21` night, does not move (#234).
- The arousal guard's two HR rules can be gated on the quality of the epoch's heart rate: `ArousalGuardConfig.min_ppg_confidence_for_hr_rule`, `ppg_quality_for_hr_rule` and `min_hr_series_quality_for_hr_rule`, the first and last as `--guard-min-ppg-confidence` and `--guard-min-hr-series-quality` on `simulate-replay-cues`, `run-pilot4-cueing` and `run-pilot5-full-night`. An epoch below a gate raises neither `sudden_heart_rate_change` nor `heart_rate_out_of_range`, does not feed the next epoch's comparison, and its decision logs `hr_rule_skipped_by` with `ppg_confidence`, `ppg_signal_quality` and `hr_series_median_quality`; non-`allow` decisions on optics presets carry those three fields too. An optics epoch whose heart-rate series skipped more than half of its values is logged as `hr_rule_blind`, `allow` included; `hr_rule_blind_action` can make it lower the volume or pause. Every default keeps today's behaviour. On synthetic input a held 15 bpm step lowers `ppg_confidence` to 0.89 on its epochs, so a gate on it holds back true jumps first, while the series' quality stays at 0.98 or more (synthetic input, not sleep). The heuristic detector adds the reason code `low_ppg_quality` when its HRV comes from PPG peaks with `ppg_confidence` below 0.4; scores and `P_REM` do not change (#234).
- The arousal guard's sudden heart-rate change rule reads a heart-rate series that `ppg_features` builds from each epoch's own optics, not the decoder's `heart_rate` stream. That stream is re-estimated from the last 5 s of `LO_NIR` on every optics notification, 36 to 77 ms apart, so a beat entering or leaving its window paused the epoch, and the 120 s cooldown the ones after it, while a true step barely moved it. The series has one value a second from 10 s windows of the most periodic optics channel band-passed to 0.5 to 4 Hz: the beat period of the window's autocorrelation, checked against the median interval between pulse maxima. A value whose two estimates differ by more than 5 bpm, or whose autocorrelation peak is below 0.5, is skipped and logged, also in `allow` decisions. A window that holds two rates, where a short surge or a step falls inside it, is read from its longest run of regular beats instead, so a surge of 15 to 40 bpm held 5 s inside an epoch pauses. The rule compares used values 10 to 20 s apart, and the guard also compares the previous epoch's values with this one's, so a step at an epoch boundary pauses the epoch after it; a step seen from both sides counts once. `sudden_hr_change_pause_bpm` now sets the bar: only changes of that size less `sudden_hr_change_margin_bpm` (1 bpm, the error of a value) count towards `sudden_hr_change_pause_count`, where before any logged change paused. `sudden_hr_change_count` and `max_sudden_hr_change_bpm` keep their names; PPG rows add `sudden_hr_change_source`, `hr_series_channel`, `hr_series_used_count`, `hr_series_skipped_count`, `hr_series_median_quality` and `hr_series_json`, `PPGFeatureConfig` adds the `hr_series_*` fields and `ArousalGuardConfig` adds `sudden_hr_change_margin_bpm` (1 bpm) and `sudden_hr_change_link_seconds` (20 s). On the four `p1034` synthetic scenarios the rule fires on 24 of 678 epochs instead of 56, 18 of them on a scripted heart-rate change of 10 bpm or more instead of 10, and the same cued puzzles play (synthetic input, not sleep). An epoch with heart-rate samples but no optics compares them as before, and `p21` still skips the HR rules. `SyntheticNightConfig.heart_rate_steps` scripts sharp heart-rate steps for tests (#247).
- The arousal guard knows when a preset has no optics. `ArousalGuardConfig.optics_capable` is set from `--preset` like the REM gate's, in `simulate-replay-cues`, `run-pilot4-cueing` and `run-pilot5-full-night`. On `p21` missing PPG no longer lowers volume or adds up to a `repeated_artifact_quality` pause, the HR rules are skipped, and each decision logs `ppg_absent_by_design`. Before this, every `p21` night was paused from the third epoch on and no cue could play. Optics presets behave as before (#234).
- The heuristic REM detector no longer weights the eye-movement proxy, which scores 1.0 in every epoch of a real night (AUC 0.500 against GSSC REM, #116). `DEFAULT_REM_WEIGHTS["eye_movement_proxy"]` is 0 and the other five are divided by 0.75 so they still sum to 1: `low_delta_power` 0.333, `theta_alpha_ratio` 0.200, `stillness` 0.267, `hr_variability` 0.133, `hr_trend` 0.067. The proxy is still scored and exported. Where it scored 1.0, `P_REM` now stays the same or goes down. The `p21` gate thresholds 0.90/0.85 were fitted with the old weights; re-grid pending (#116, #225).
- EEG feature rows count eye-movement deflections on AF7 − AF8 filtered to 0.3 to 5 Hz: `rem_event_count`, `rem_density_per_min`, `last_rem_event_age_s`, `rem_burst_active`, `rem_event_times`, `rem_event_threshold_uv` and `rem_event_warmup`, from `RemEventTracker` in `muse_tmr.features.rem_event_features`. The threshold is max(5 × MAD, 10 µV) over the last 5 min, and one tracker runs per recording in `simulate-replay-cues`, `run-pilot4-cueing`, `run-pilot5-full-night`, `annotate-template` and `scripts/validate_rem_detection.py`. The heuristic detector scores the density as `rem_event_density` (`eeg_rem_events_support`/`eeg_rem_events_low`) at weight 0, with UNFITTED thresholds, so `P_REM` does not change. `eye_movement_proxy` stays as a deprecated column. On the synthetic fixture (synthetic input, not sleep) the old proxy scores 1.0 on REM-dense and REM-free epochs alike, while the new count finds 60 of 60 injected deflections in each of five 5 min sessions, with and without a 40 µV line at 63.93 Hz (#225).
- The REM gate keeps real time at any `--stride-seconds`. `simulate-replay-cues`, `run-pilot4-cueing` and `run-pilot5-full-night` pass it the time between epoch starts (`epoch_step_seconds()`, one stride for the first epoch) instead of the 30 s epoch length, so at a 10 s stride the 60 s stable window and the 120 s cooldown no longer run three times too fast. At the default 30 s stride a replay gives the same scheduler events as before. Pilot 3 metrics and `pilot4_summary.json` add `stride_seconds`, `first_gate_open_seconds` and `first_play_seconds`. On a synthetic NREM-to-REM stream (synthetic input, not sleep) the first cue came 60 to 85 s after REM onset at a 30 s stride, 80 to 85 s at 10 s and 85 s at 5 s, so a shorter stride does not bring it earlier with the gate as it is (#225, #167).
- A night that asks for sound no longer runs silently. With `--backend system` or `afplay` and no `afplay` on PATH, `play-test-cue`, `train-tlr-cue`, `run-pilot4-cueing` and `run-pilot5-full-night` exit with code 2 and say how to fix it; the two night commands do so before they read an input, connect the headband or write a file. Before this `system` quietly became `dry-run` and the night could still pass. `--allow-dry-run` (`Pilot4CueingConfig.allow_dry_run_fallback`) runs silently on purpose. Pilot 4/5 `metadata.json` adds `requested_backend_name` and `resolved_backend_name`, the summary `requested_audio_backend_name` and `resolved_audio_backend_name`. `create_audio_backend()` is unchanged; the commands use the new `resolve_audio_backend()` (#232).

### Reports

- `muse-tmr score-cue-arousal <night>` scores what followed each cue. For every scheduler `play` it compares the 10 s after the cue with the 20 s before it: alpha (8 to 12 Hz), beta (15 to 30 Hz) and 55 to 95 Hz EMG-band power rising at least 2 times, with the 64 Hz optics line fitted out of each 1 s segment and bridged; an arousal-proxy movement from 2 s before to 10 s after; and a jump of 10 bpm in the optics heart-rate series of #251, never the decoder's `heart_rate` stream. Each cue gets a quiet control moment in the same gate state, more than 60 s from every `play`, scored the same way, so the rate per cue stands next to the night's background rate. It reads the night's logs, replays `raw_amused.bin` once, with BLE gaps as missing samples, while frames that only came late, as the subpacket counter shows, keep their place, and writes `cue_arousal_events.jsonl`, `cue_arousal_report.json` and `.md`: the rate per flag for cues and controls, movement events per cue and per control, the cue-attributed awakening rate, the share of REM periods (gate-open runs) with a disruption, REM termination within 60 s of a cue or a control, and dose tables by effective volume from `audio_playback.jsonl`, time since the previous cue and cue index, with the guard's action and multiplier per cue. These are arousal proxies, not scored arousals: the thresholds are UNFITTED, the volume is a software gain, and no validator fails on them. `run-pilot4-cueing` and `run-pilot5-full-night` run it on their own night when it ends and add a `cue_arousal` block to the summary, or `failed` with the reason; `score-cue-arousal` on the night's directory, after awakening markers were added in the morning, rewrites that block. They now play each cue on a worker thread, so the headband's packets keep the time they came while a tone plays; on the event loop every packet of a tone was stamped when it ended. Pilot 4 and 5 write each epoch's gate decision to `rem_gate_decisions.jsonl`, and their summary, schema 2, adds `first_epoch_start` and `rem_gate_decisions_path`; Pilot 3 metrics add `first_epoch_start`. `muse_tmr.validation.gate_log` reads either log and defines a REM period as a gate-open run, for this scorer and the protocol fidelity report to come. `simulate-replay-cues --inject-cues-every-seconds N` adds synthetic cues to gate-open epochs, outside the scheduler, to check where the windows fall on a replayed night; the scheduler events stay the same. `log-pilot4-awakening` takes `--timestamp-seconds` and `--attributed-to-cue`, and older markers load as before. On a 45 min `clean_night` (synthetic input, not sleep) cues at its 7 arousals outside wake all flag and 1 of 18 at quiet moments does. No night with sleep-time audio has been scored yet (#227).
- `muse-tmr analyze-optics-agreement <recording>` compares the heart rate the Muse takes from its optics with the R-peaks of a Polar H10 ECG recorded with it on `p1034`: the decoder's `heart_rate` stream, `ppg_estimated_hr_bpm` and each optics channel per 30 s epoch, and the heart-rate series per 10 s window, as bias, SD, 95 % limits of agreement and MAE by `ppg_signal_quality`, `ppg_confidence` and the series' quality. It also runs the sudden heart-rate change rule as the guard does and scores its pauses against the ECG change between the same windows, with what a quality gate would keep. It writes `optics_agreement.json` and `.md` under `data/reports/optics_agreement/`, and exits with code 2 without `polar/`, without ECG or on `p21`. `ppg_features.estimate_channel_heart_rates()` gives the per-channel estimates, with a periodicity that, unlike `ppg_confidence`, does not rate band-passed noise as a pulse. Agreement with a chest ECG, a proxy; no paired night yet (#234).
- The REM report is built by `muse_tmr.reports.nightly_report`. `scripts/generate_nightly_report.py` and the app's Build report run it as before, and `muse-tmr demo` builds it from an installed wheel. A recording whose `metadata.json` says `synthetic: true` gets a "Synthetic input, not sleep" callout at the top and the note in its subtitle, and `validate-pilot1-recording` and `simulate-replay-cues` add `"synthetic": true` and the note to the report they save and the output they print (#233).

### Development and docs

## 0.2.0 (2026-10-19)

### Local app

- The app is called Athena TMR and shares the meditation guide's look. The Record tab puts the headband on the left and the five recording modes on the right, each saying what it records; a running recording shows its progress, battery, EEG rate and lost subpackets; the Recordings tab lists every recording with its reports and the same EEG numbers. The page, the guide and the macOS launcher share one icon (#185).
- A Morning tab for the day after a puzzle night: the dream report and a blind, timed retest, saved through the same code as `record-dream-report` and `record-puzzle-retest` and never showing which puzzles were cued (#186).
- During a recording the browser tab shows its time, `muse-tmr app --help` and the runbooks use the new name, and reports opened from the app get its icon (#200).

## 0.1.0 (2026-10-19)

The first tagged version. It covers what `main` could do on that date.

### Recording

- Overnight and short recordings from the Muse S Athena over BLE through the forked amused-py layer: EEG, IMU, PPG optics, heart rate and battery, with reconnects, a `progress.json` heartbeat and a `summary.json` at the end.
- Every BLE notification is kept as received in `raw_amused.bin`, so replay runs the same decoder as the live stream, from 0x to 20x speed.
- The decoder drops repeated subpackets and marks lost ones as gaps in `EEGSample` and `PPGSample` without filling them in (#182).
- OpenMuse (LSL) and BrainFlow sources produce the same `MuseFrame`. A Polar H10 chest strap can record ECG, RR and chest motion alongside, in its own process.

### Local app

- Live contact for TP9, AF7, AF8 and TP10 with a headband view and a check mark when all four are good (#187), headset scan, battery and decoder diagnostics.
- Session (1 h, p1034), night (8 h, p21), guided meditation, meditation series and calibration recordings, each run as its own `muse-tmr record` process under `caffeinate`.
- A start waits until all four channels have been good for 5 s, and the server refuses a start before that (#189).
- Reports are built from the page, recent recordings stay listed, and the app updates itself from `main` when idle. A macOS launcher installs it on the Desktop.

### REM detection and cueing

- 30-second epochs with coverage and quality flags; EEG band powers and ratios, an eye-movement proxy, IMU motion and arousal proxies, PPG heart rate and HRV proxies.
- A heuristic REM detector with reason codes, and a personal logistic regression classifier trained on annotated epochs and saved as versioned JSON.
- A stable REM gate with preset-dependent thresholds (0.90/0.85 on p21), an arousal guard, and a cue scheduler that runs a TLR block first and then only the cued puzzle cues, played under calibrated volume caps with fades and an emergency stop.
- `scripts/validate_rem_detection.py` scores the detector and the gate against YASA or GSSC staging.

### Protocol

- Puzzle catalogs, timed attempts, seeded cued and uncued assignment, cue association checks and TLR cue training.
- Morning dream reports, blind puzzle retests and the cued-vs-uncued analysis.
- Five staged pilots, each with a runbook and a validator.

### Meditation side track

- A/B sessions in ABBA or BAAB order with a tone and a voice cue in English or Russian at each change, and stability, sensory fading and drowsiness ratings per block.
- An HTML report with complexity metrics, an EMG check and, with the H10, breathing and HRV; series aggregation with p-values from 8 sessions on; a 23-minute voice-guided calibration run.

### Packaging and docs

- Installs as `athena-tmr-lucid` with every `muse_tmr` subpackage; CI builds the wheel and checks it (#188).
- A first-recording quick start in the README, runnable feature examples in `docs/USAGE.md`, and a short agent rules page (#190).
