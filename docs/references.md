# References

The papers the protocol and the REM check lean on, with what each one is used for here. The notes behind this list, with the figures checked against the full texts, are in [`research/2026-10-uniqueness/notes/`](research/2026-10-uniqueness/notes/) (`tmr_tlr_science.md`, `verify_tmr_tlr_science.md`, `gap_2_the_repo_s_only_accuracy_figures_live_so.md`). Citing a paper here does not mean REMind reproduces its result: no night with sleep-time audio has been run yet.

## Cueing in REM sleep and lucidity training

- Konkoly KR, Appel K, Chabani E, et al. Real-time dialogue between experimenters and dreamers during REM sleep. *Current Biology* 31:1417–1427 (2021). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.01.026, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8162929/. Lucid dreamers in PSG-verified REM signalled with left-right eye movements and answered questions. REMind has no eye-signal detection and no two-way channel, which is why its protocol is described as one-way. Its four groups give four presets in [`protocol_presets.md`](protocol_presets.md); the STAR Methods were read in full for them.
- Carr M, Konkoly K, Mallett R, Edwards C, Appel K, Blagrove M. Combining presleep cognitive training and REM-sleep stimulation in a laboratory morning nap for lucid dream induction. *Psychology of Consciousness: Theory, Research, and Practice* 10(4):413–430 (online 2020, issue 2023). https://doi.org/10.1037/cns0000227. Pre-sleep cue training followed by the same cue in REM, the pattern the TLR block follows.
- Konkoly KR, Whitmore NW, Mallett R, Mazurek CY, Paller KA. Provoking lucid dreams at home with sensory cues paired with pre-sleep cognitive training. *Consciousness and Cognition* 125:103759 (2024). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2024.103759, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11542932. The presets `konkoly2024_exp1` and `konkoly2024_exp2`, from the full text. A home app without sleep monitoring; its randomized second experiment had no-cue and untrained-cue control nights, and cue-induced awakenings were frequent. Movement events per cue were 0.19 against 0.10 in silent periods, the comparison `safety_outcomes.md` copies with paired quiet controls.
- Konkoly KR, Morris DJ, Hurka K, Martinez AM, Sanders KEG, Paller KA. Creative problem-solving after experimentally provoking dreams of unsolved puzzles during REM sleep. *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2026(1):niaf067 (2026). https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niaf067, https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2026/1/niaf067/8456489, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12875123/. Puzzle soundtracks played in REM to frequent lucid dreamers in the lab; no overall cueing effect on solving, cues entered dreams. The preset `konkoly2026_puzzle` and the morning sound check, from the full text in the PMC copy.
- Picard-Deland C, Jafarzadeh Esfahani M, et al. Inducing lucid dreaming with multisensory stimulation: a preregistered multi-center study. *bioRxiv* 2024.06.21.600133, v2 (2026), preprint, not peer reviewed. https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.21.600133. Signal-verified lucid dreams did not differ between cueing and sham in lab naps; cues disrupted sleep in about a quarter of cued REM periods (23.7 %). Version 1 (Jafarzadeh Esfahani M, Salvesen LAC, Picard-Deland C, et al., 2024, "Highly effective verified lucid dream induction using combined cognitive-sensory training and wearable EEG: a multi-centre study", https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.21.600133v1.full) gives the cueing parameters of the preset `dresler2024_multisensory`, read in full.

## Arousals after cues

[`safety_outcomes.md`](safety_outcomes.md) scores arousal proxies after each cue against quiet controls because these studies report arousals after cues as the safety outcome. Their figures come from PSG or other devices and are a sense of scale, not a target.

- Whitmore N, Paller KA. *Learning & Memory* 30(3):63–69 (2023). https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.053615.122, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10027237. In the lab, AASM-scored arousals within 10 s followed 57.9 % of loud (about 43 dBA) and 43.0 % of soft (about 28 dBA) cues during sleep (arousal analyses on 23 participants), and items cued with an arousal were forgotten more than uncued ones; the authors conclude that the degree of arousal matters more than the objective intensity of the cue.
- Whitmore N, et al. *Journal of Sleep Research* 31:e13731 (2022). https://doi.org/10.1111/jsr.13731. SleepStim, a home system on a phone and a fitness tracker that lowered its sound intensity when the sleep-stage probability fell during sounds; its code is home-tmr.
- Coon et al. *Frontiers in Neuroscience* 19:1682450 (2026). https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2025.1682450, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12914948/. Recommendations for closed-loop acoustic stimulation, among them stricter settings in REM sleep and a log of every stimulus and arousal.

The Konkoly et al. 2024 and Picard-Deland et al. 2024 entries above also report arousal outcomes: 0.19 movement events per cue against 0.10 in silent periods (Konkoly), and sleep disrupted in 23.7 % of cued REM periods (Picard-Deland, preprint).

## Targeted memory reactivation

- Sanders KEG, Osburn S, Paller KA, Beeman M. Targeted memory reactivation during sleep improves next-day problem solving. *Psychological Science* 30(11):1616–1624 (2019). https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797619873344, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6843748. Correction: *Psychological Science* 31(8):1048 (2020), not read. The cued and uncued puzzle design with a morning retest and the sound-to-puzzle recall test before it; its cues were played in slow-wave sleep, not REM, so its preset is documentation only.
- Schwartz S, Clerget A, Perogamvros L. Enhancing imagery rehearsal therapy for nightmares with targeted memory reactivation. *Current Biology* 32:4808–4816 (2022). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2022.09.032. Home REM cueing with an automatic stage-detecting headband in a patient group. The preset `schwartz2022_irt_rem` is from the research notes only.
- Borghese F, Henckaerts P, Guy F, Perez Mayo C, Delplanque S, Schwartz S, Perogamvros L. Targeted memory reactivation during REM sleep in patients with social anxiety disorder. *Frontiers in Psychiatry* 13:904704 (2022). https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2022.904704, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9281560/. Home REM cueing with a headband that paused cues after movement, alpha, blinks and poor signal, comparable to what the arousal guard does. The preset `borghese2022_rem_home`, from the full text.
- Pereira, Santamaria, Andrews, Schmidt, Van Rossum and Lewis. *Journal of Neuroscience* 43(21):3838–3848 (2023). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10218979/. REM cueing helped rule abstraction at a retest one week later, not the next day; why the Morning tab and `record-puzzle-retest --wave` have a `day7` retest ([`nof1_protocol.md`](nof1_protocol.md)). From the research notes (`verify_tmr_tlr_science.md` row 12), full text not read for this page.
- Rakowska et al. *NeuroImage* (2021). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8591408/. A TMR benefit in NREM sleep at 10 days, not at 24 h or 6 weeks; the `day10` retest. From the research notes (`tmr_tlr_science.md`), full text not read for this page.
- Salfi F, D'Atri A, Arnone B, et al. Promoting vocabulary learning during sleep at home using closed-loop targeted memory reactivation. *Journal of Sleep Research* 34(6):e70000 (2025). https://doi.org/10.1111/jsr.70000, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12592824/. Home TMR in slow-wave sleep with the Dreem headband Borghese et al. used; the authors write that its stimulation system was discontinued, so that home setup cannot be run again as it was.

## Sleep staging used as a reference

- Hanna J, Flöel A. An accessible and versatile deep learning-based sleep stage classifier. *Frontiers in Neuroinformatics* 17:1086634 (2023). https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fninf.2023.1086634/full. GSSC: trained on 3,314 NSRR PSG nights, mostly on C3, C4, F3, F4, and able to stage from one EEG channel. There is no published evaluation on forehead-only EEG such as the Muse AF7/AF8.
- Hanif U, et al. Deciphering insomnia: benchmarking automated sleep staging algorithms for complex sleep disorders. *Journal of Sleep Research* 34(6):e70048 (2025). https://doi.org/10.1111/jsr.70048. GSSC ahead of YASA on clinical PSG (REM F1 0.76 against 0.35).
- Gunter KM, et al. *medRxiv* 2026.07.06.26357378 (2026), preprint, not peer reviewed. https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.06.26357378v1.full. Open stagers on a portable device with one frontal EEG channel plus EOG and EMG; GSSC was the most robust.
- Lanthier M, et al. *Sleep Advances* zpaf089 (2025). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12782022/. The vendor's closed algorithm on Muse-S against PSG: five-class κ 0.76, REM κ 0.85. A different measure from the binary REM-vs-not κ that `scripts/validate_rem_detection.py` reports, and not comparable with it.
- Jafarzadeh Esfahani M, et al. *bioRxiv* 2023.08.18.553744 (2023), preprint. https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.08.18.553744v1. ZMax staging; an example of how undisclosed training data makes a stager hard to compare. On ZMax's F7 and F8, referenced to Fpz, an automatic detector's REM eye-movement counts correlated r = 0.66 with its counts on PSG EOG and came out about 1.8 times as high on average, with a wide spread; [`synthetic_data.md`](synthetic_data.md) cites it for why the synthetic eye-movement deflections are an assumption.
- Parry YD, Briganti G. *medRxiv* 2026.06.01.26354591 (2026), preprint. https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.01.26354591v1.full. Home forehead EEG staging on 100 nights; a forehead-trained stager could not be evaluated because it would not install.

## The synthetic optics pulse

[`synthetic_data.md`](synthetic_data.md) builds the optics of a synthetic night from these. The widths and levels come from the maintainer's own recordings, not from the papers.

- Allen J. Photoplethysmography and its application in clinical physiological measurement. *Physiological Measurement* 28(3):R1–R39 (2007). https://doi.org/10.1088/0967-3334/28/3/R01. The pulse as a small cardiac part on a large, slowly varying level that breathing, sympathetic activity and thermoregulation move: here a pulse on a DC level with a wandering baseline.
- Elgendi M. On the analysis of fingertip photoplethysmogram signals. *Current Cardiology Reviews* 8(1):14–25 (2012). https://doi.org/10.2174/157340312801215782. The pulse described by its systolic and diastolic waves.
- Tang Q, Chen Z, Ward R, Elgendi M. Synthetic photoplethysmogram generation using two Gaussian functions. *Scientific Reports* 10:13883 (2020). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-69076-x. Synthetic beats as the sum of a systolic and a diastolic Gaussian wave. The generator makes the systolic wave log-normal and adds a narrow dip at the pulse foot for the sharp light maximum the recordings show.

## Agreement of the optics heart rate with an ECG

[`optics_agreement.md`](optics_agreement.md) reports how far the Muse optics heart rate is from a Polar H10 ECG this way.

- Bland JM, Altman DG. Statistical methods for assessing agreement between two methods of clinical measurement. *The Lancet* 327(8476):307–310 (1986). https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(86)90837-8. Agreement as the mean difference between two measures and the 95 % limits of agreement, the mean plus and minus 1.96 SD of the differences, instead of a correlation.
